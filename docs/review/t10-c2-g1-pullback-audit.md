## Public interface

With `Set Printing Implicit`, the final public types are:

```coq
@F_Z
  : forall (d : nat) (generators : list (ZVec d)),
      ZVec (@length (ZVec d) generators) -> ZVec d

@F_Q
  : forall (d : nat) (generators : list (ZVec d)),
      QVec (@length (ZVec d) generators) -> QVec d

vcone_pullback_finitely_generated
  : forall (d : nat) (groups : list (ZVec d))
      (cones : list (QVec d)),
      exists generators : list (QVec (@length (ZVec d) groups)),
        forall z : QVec (@length (ZVec d) groups),
          @qcone_generated (@length (ZVec d) groups) generators z <->
          @qcone_generated d cones (@F_Q d groups z)
```

`F_Z` is the existing integer-coefficient `zgroup_combination`; `F_Q` is its
rational-coefficient extension through `qgroup_combination`.  The adapter also
proves `F_Z_add`, `F_Z_scale`, `F_Q_of_Z`, `F_Q_add`, `F_Q_scale`, and
`F_Q_linear_combination`, covering integer embedding, addition, scalar
multiplication, and finite linear combinations.

## Mathematical connection

The proof constructs an H-system over `(z, lambda)` with
`length cones + 2*d` rows:

- the first `length cones` rows impose `lambda_j >= 0`;
- the next `d` rows impose `F_Q(z)_i - sum_j lambda_j v_j_i >= 0`;
- the final `d` rows impose the opposite inequalities.

Thus no coordinatewise nonnegativity condition is imposed on `z`.  The two
equality rows are combined using `Qle_antisym`.  The equivalence between cone
membership and an occurrence-aligned nonnegative coefficient family is derived
from `qcone_generated_aligned_coefficients`; duplicate generators require no
decidable vector equality.

`rational_hcone_finitely_generated` supplies a fixed finite generator list in
the auxiliary space.  `qcone_project_left` proves both directions between its
projection and the projected generator list.  Projection is proved compatible
with addition, nonnegative scaling, and `QVecEq`.

For `cones = []`, the H-system has no lambda rows and retains exactly the two
inequalities expressing `F_Q(z) = 0`.  The audit includes the nonzero kernel
point `(1,-1)` for the map generated by `[1;1]`, preventing an accidental
replacement of the kernel by `{0}`.

## Boundary regression

The focused examples cover:

- dimension zero;
- an empty group-generator list;
- an empty cone-generator list and a nonzero kernel point;
- a zero map;
- duplicated and linearly dependent group/cone generators;
- a nonpointed cone with lineality.

The prior G0, G1-I, and G1-Hilbert `.vo` targets and their audit examples were
also rebuilt directly through `Makefile.coq`, avoiding modification of their
existing uncommitted audit logs.
